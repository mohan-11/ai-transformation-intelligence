# Value Chain Guide

The value chain (Porter, 1985) decomposes any organisation into the activities
that create value. It is deliberately generic so it applies to any industry.

## Primary Activities
- **Inbound Logistics** — receiving, storing and distributing inputs.
- **Operations** — transforming inputs into the product or service.
- **Outbound Logistics** — collecting, storing and delivering to customers.
- **Marketing & Sales** — persuading customers to buy.
- **Service** — maintaining value after the sale (support, repair, training).

## Support Activities
- **Procurement** — acquiring the resources the organisation needs.
- **Technology & IT** — infrastructure, systems, data and innovation.
- **Human Resources** — recruiting, training and developing people.
- **Firm Infrastructure** — finance, legal, planning and governance.

## Using this framework
Map each business process to the value-chain area it belongs to, then look for
where AI capability patterns (see the AI Capability Guide) can remove manual
effort, reduce errors, speed decisions or improve outcomes. Prioritise where
business value is high, data is available, and implementation is feasible.
